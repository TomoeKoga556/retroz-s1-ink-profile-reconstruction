# Expectation Versus Result

## What was expected

Measured reference edges were broader and asymmetric, so a local deterministic reconstruction seemed likely to move modern raster contours toward those statistics while preserving p50 and color plateaus.

## Why it was plausible

The target direction was stable across five folds, analytical edge models were available, and early synthetic fixtures showed controllable half-profile movement. Manual labels were expected to remove the last localization uncertainty.

## What happened

- A did not establish safe activation.
- B moved part of the dark side but failed the bright side, total width, and fold stability.
- C-family operators produced artifacts or invalid evidence.
- D3 passed its synthetic contracts and then returned exact real no-op outputs.
- The Manual Oracle produced local nonzero changes that remained invisible in all 50 frozen native comparisons.

## Why the expectation failed

The measured gap did not identify a unique safe image operation. Raster eligibility, plateau evidence, junction protection, and perceived contour stability consumed the apparent headroom. Perfect localization did not make the evaluated renderer family visually useful.

## What came next

Each failure produced reusable infrastructure: stricter activation tests, independent raster measurement, source-fixed geometry, sandboxed evaluation, provenance closure, and finally a clean separation between technical nonzero output and perceptual usefulness.
